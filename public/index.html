00Summary

The opportunity

A corner site at Grand Street and Graham Avenue, one block from the Grand St L station, in a C4-4A district that follows R7A rules. Zoning allows 4.0 FAR as of right and 5.01 with Inclusionary Housing certificates, which this block can still buy because it sits in the former Greenpoint/Williamsburg IH designated area. Two things make the site unusual: the neighbor at 727 Grand has unused air rights that only this lot can practically buy, and the existing 1930 building can be kept and built on top of, which takes demolition and several thousand square feet of new construction out of the budget. The ground floor stays commercial, and the whole page below is a working model — change any assumption and every number moves.

Z-001Site

North side of Grand Street, east corner of Graham Avenue

725 Grand St is a 3,034 sf irregular corner lot, 25.17 ft on Grand and 100 ft along Graham. Its rear arm (25.83 × 20 ft) runs behind 727 Grand St, a 25.83 × 80 ft interior lot. Together they form a 51 × 100 ft rectangle of 5,101 sf. The plan is drawn with Grand Street at the top, so north points down.

Both streets are wide (80 ft) on the city's street map, so every setback above the base height is 10 ft (ZR 23-433). The whole merged lot is within 100 ft of the corner, so it's all corner lot: 100% coverage and no rear yard.

Neighbor's floor area. Your field measurement is a full 25.83 × 80 ft ground floor plus two floors of 25.83 × 40 ft, about 4,133 sf, which leaves a 4,135 sf transfer. PLUTO lists 6,042 sf (3 floors × 2,014 sf), which would cut the transfer to about 2,226 sf. The CO or a survey will settle it. You can switch between the two below.

E-232 (2009 rezoning): Phase I/II hazardous materials testing, plus window-wall noise attenuation with alternate ventilation. Expect DEP/OER sign-off and a closed-window design with mechanical ventilation.

Certificates: the UAP off-site option

  • The site is in the former Greenpoint/Williamsburg IH designated area (adopted 2009), which ZR 27-111 makes a UAP off-site option area. A UAP development there can go above FAR 4.0 "by utilizing affordable housing provided on a generating site," at the pre-2024 rate in ZR 23-154, as long as the generating site vested under ZR 27-132.
  • A UAP development counts as "qualifying affordable housing" (ZR 12-10), so as I read it the 85 ft base and 115 ft maximum of ZR 23-432 apply even with no affordable units on site. Confirm with land-use counsel
  • Bonus needed: —. At the old IH designated area ratio of 1.25 sf of bonus per sf of affordable housing, that's about — of generating-site affordable floor area. Supply is limited, and the off-site option ends around Dec 2034.
Z-004Deductions

What counts toward FAR, and what doesn't

Exempt

Recessed loggias

A balcony or terrace is excluded if no more than 67% of its perimeter is enclosed. For a recess open on one side, that means width at least equal to depth. The studies use 6 × 10 ft loggias and 8 × 12 ft corner loggias. At 8 ft deep or less they also stay within the 8 ft street wall tolerance.

ZR 12-10 floor area · 23-43
Exempt

Green building: 5% of floor area

For a fully electrified or ultra-low-energy building (15% better than the Energy Code, LL154, with commissioning).

ZR 12-10
Exempt

Amenities up to 5% of residential floor area

Roof lounge above grade. Fitness, packages and bikes go in the cellar, where they don't count against the 5%.

ZR 23-231 · 23-63
Exempt

Corridors: 50% or 100%

50% when the corridor is under 100 ft long. Another 50% when it ends at a 3BR or is daylit; C and D qualify above 30 ft.

ZR 23-232 Confirm stacking
Exempt

Mechanical, cellar, bulkheads, refuse

Rooftop mechanical on the core arm (up to 30% of coverage, 35 ft above max height). Refuse rooms up to 3 sf per unit.

ZR 12-10 · 23-412 · 23-233
Counts*

Lobby, retail, stairs, elevator

Read literally, ZR 12-10 and 23-232 exempt corridors, not lobbies. The studies default to your assumption that the lobby and elevator lobbies are 100% exempt. Switch "Circulation deduction" to code-literal to see the conservative case. Get a DOB/expediter determination

Ground-floor retail condo

Commercial condos sell to investors on in-place income, so a signed lease before sale supports the 5% cap rate. ICAP (Industrial & Commercial Abatement Program) phases in the tax increase from new construction; apply before permits are pulled verify eligibility.

Average condo $/sf is the headline price: the blended rate across all sellable area, and the number the whole model is driven by. The four band prices below it are relativities — lower floors, middle floors, upper floors, penthouse. They are rescaled so that their area-weighted average always equals the average you set, which is why they settle on numbers slightly either side of what you type: raise the average and all four rise with it; type over one band to change its relationship to the others and the rest absorb the difference. Band sizes say how many floors sit in each band, counted from the top down, with the middle band taking whatever is left. Every one of these is saved with the scenario. Condo sales costs cover brokerage, marketing, closing costs and the sponsor-paid NYC/NYS transfer taxes. Loggias and terraces are priced at $0 by default, so the band prices apply to interior area only. Enter an outdoor premium to value them.

F-300Returns

Return on investment

The bank funds part of the total project cost, including land, acquisitions and its own interest reserve. Equity covers the rest. The loan is repaid from sales, and profit is what remains after the loan and every cost. Interest is estimated on the average drawn balance over the full project period.
